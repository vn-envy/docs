A referral from someone inside your target company can be the difference between an application that disappears into an ATS and one that reaches a hiring manager's desk. Rolecrft generates the complete outreach toolkit you need — from finding the right people on LinkedIn to writing an email that gets a response — all calibrated to your target company and role. You'll find everything on the **Launch Pad** tab of your results dashboard, under the Networking section.

## LinkedIn Target Contacts

Rolecrft generates specific LinkedIn search query strings to help you find three types of people who can help your application:

- **Potential referrers**: Current employees in the same team or adjacent function as your target role.
- **Hiring managers**: People who carry a title one or two levels above the role you're applying for, in the same department.
- **Alumni from your background**: People who made the same career transition or came from the same previous company — the most likely to respond to a cold message.

Each search query is written to paste directly into the LinkedIn search bar.

**Example search query:**

```text
"Software Engineer" OR "SWE" site:linkedin.com/in
  company:"Stripe" location:"Bangalore OR Bengaluru"
  -intern -student
```

<Tip>
  LinkedIn's free tier limits search result depth. If you hit a wall, try narrowing the company name to the specific subsidiary or business unit, or search by job title without the company filter and scan profiles manually for the company name in the experience section.
</Tip>

## Connection Request Message

Rolecrft writes a ≤280-character connection request message for each type of contact you're reaching out to. The character limit is deliberate — LinkedIn's connection request note field has a hard 300-character cap, and shorter messages are more likely to be accepted.

The message follows a three-part structure:

1. **Credibility anchor**: One sentence establishing who you are and a specific, genuine reason for reaching out to this person (not "I'm interested in opportunities at your company").
2. **Ask**: A specific, low-friction ask — not "Can you refer me?" but "Would you be open to a 15-minute call to share your experience on the team?"
3. **No-pressure close**: A brief opt-out that makes accepting feel easy.

**Example connection request:**

> Hi [Name] — I'm a payments PM with 6 years at Razorpay and I'm exploring roles at Stripe. Your post on API design for Stripe Billing caught my attention. Would you be open to a 15-min chat? Happy to work around your schedule.

## Cold Hiring Manager Email

Rolecrft drafts a cold email to a hiring manager — the person most likely to push your application forward if they're impressed. The email has four components:

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Subject line">
    Specific and credibility-first. Avoids words that trigger spam filters ("opportunity", "application", "job"). Uses your most impressive single credential and the exact role title.

    **Example**: *Payments PM (6 yrs Razorpay) — Senior PM, Stripe Billing*
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Opening paragraph — the hook">
    Two to three sentences. States who you are, your most relevant credential, and why you're reaching out to this person specifically. References something concrete about their team's work to prove this isn't a mass email.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Body paragraph — the evidence">
    Three to four sentences matching your strongest relevant experience to the JD's most important requirements. Includes at least one quantified achievement. Does not paste your resume.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Closing paragraph — the ask">
    One specific, easy ask: a 20-minute call, a quick reply to a single question, or permission to submit an application with their name. Provides two specific time options in their timezone. Includes a one-sentence opt-out.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Follow-Up Message

After your connection request is accepted, Rolecrft provides a follow-up message to send before asking for anything. This message adds context about your background and opens a natural conversation — giving the contact a reason to engage before you make any ask.

## Conversation Guide for Informational Chats

When someone agrees to an informational chat, the goal is not to ask for a job — it's to build a genuine connection that leads to a referral organically. Rolecrft provides a four-part conversation guide:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Openers (first 3 minutes)">
    Two or three opening questions that show you've done your research on the company and the person's specific team. Avoids generic questions like "What's the culture like?" in favour of specific ones like "I noticed your team shipped [product feature] — how did that change your team's roadmap priorities?"
  </Step>

  <Step title="Bridges (middle 10 minutes)">
    Transition questions that naturally move from the contact's experience to yours. These create space for you to share relevant context about your background without making it feel like an interview.

    **Example bridge**: *"That's really interesting — the challenge of [thing they described] sounds similar to what I faced at [your company] when [brief context]. Did you find that [follow-up question]?"*
  </Step>

  <Step title="The ask (final 5 minutes)">
    A clean, specific ask that does not put the contact in an uncomfortable position. The best ask is for a referral to apply — not a job offer, not an introduction to the hiring manager.

    **Example ask**: *"I'm seriously considering applying for the [role] position. If you think the role might be a good fit based on what we've talked about, I'd really appreciate a referral through the internal system — it doesn't commit you to anything beyond that."*
  </Step>

  <Step title="Decline pivot">
    If the contact says they don't feel comfortable referring someone they've just met, don't push. Rolecrft provides a graceful pivot that keeps the relationship warm: thank them for their time, ask one final question that shows genuine interest in their career, and leave the door open for a follow-up message after you've applied directly.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Use this section to build a parallel pipeline — applying to similar companies simultaneously multiplies your chances without multiplying your prep time.

## Timing Strategy

Rolecrft includes a timing strategy note on the Networking section that recommends when to send each type of outreach relative to your application timeline:

- **LinkedIn connection request**: Send 5–7 days before you plan to apply. This gives the contact time to accept and have a brief exchange before you mention the application.
- **Cold hiring manager email**: Send the same day you submit your application, or the day before. Hiring managers who receive an email and then see an application in their ATS are significantly more likely to open the application.
- **Informational chat follow-up**: Send a thank-you message within 24 hours. Mention one specific thing you learned from the conversation to make it memorable.

<Warning>
  Do not send the connection request and the cold email to the same person simultaneously. Pick one channel per contact. Sending both at once signals inexperience and reduces the likelihood of a response.
</Warning>
