Getting an interview is only the first hurdle. Rolecrft's interview preparation tools help you show up ready with two things that take most candidates days to assemble manually: structured STAR stories drawn from your own experience, and a question bank calibrated to the specific role and company you're targeting. Both are generated from the same resume and JD data you already provided — no extra input required. You'll find everything on the **Flight Plan** tab of your results dashboard.

## STAR Interview Stories

Behavioral interviews rely on the STAR framework — Situation, Task, Action, Result. Rolecrft generates complete STAR narratives from your actual experience as recorded in your resume, not from generic templates. Each story is grounded in a real achievement you described and then shaped into the structured format interviewers expect.

### What Each STAR Story Contains

Every story Rolecrft produces has five components:

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Full STAR Narrative">
    A 120–150 word narrative structured across all four STAR components. Written in first person, past tense, active voice.

    **Situation**: Sets the context — company stage, team size, business problem, or project scope. Specific enough to be credible, brief enough not to waste interview time.

    **Task**: What you personally were responsible for achieving. Distinguishes your individual mandate from the broader team goal.

    **Action**: The specific steps you took. Uses strong action verbs. Focuses on decisions you made and skills you applied, not outcomes that happened to you.

    **Result**: Quantified outcomes where your resume provides metrics, or qualitative outcomes where metrics aren't available. Connects the result to business impact.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Spoken Version (60–80 words)">
    A condensed, conversational version of the same story — natural enough to deliver without sounding rehearsed. Use this when an interviewer asks an opening question like "Tell me about a time when..." and you need to answer concisely before they ask a follow-up.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Metric Math">
    If your resume contains a metric (e.g., "reduced latency by 40%"), Rolecrft shows the supporting calculation — what the before and after numbers imply, and how to defend the figure if an interviewer probes it. If the metric came from a [SUGGESTED] tag, this section reminds you to verify the number before using it.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Skills Demonstrated">
    A tag list of the competencies this story evidences — for example: `cross-functional leadership`, `data-driven decision making`, `stakeholder management`. Use this to mentally cross-reference which story to pull for each interview question.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

### Exporting STAR Stories

Export all STAR stories as a CSV from the **Flight Plan** tab. The CSV contains one row per story with columns for each STAR component, the spoken version, the metric math, and the skills demonstrated. Use the CSV to create a personal story bank in Notion, Sheets, or any tool you prefer for interview prep.

## Interview Question Bank

Rolecrft generates 8–10 interview questions tailored to your specific target role and company. Questions are not pulled from a generic pool — they are constructed from the JD responsibilities, the company's publicly known interview culture, and the seniority level of the role.

### Question Categories

Question categories are calibrated to your role type. For engineering roles, the four dimensions are:

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="DSA & Coding">
    Algorithm and data structure questions calibrated to the complexity level expected for your target role. A mid-level engineer role at a fintech startup gets different DSA questions than a staff engineer role at a large tech company.

    Each question includes: **difficulty** (Easy / Medium / Hard), **key concepts** to review, and **likely follow-ups** the interviewer may ask after your initial solution.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="System Design">
    Open-ended system design questions grounded in the company's product domain. A payments company gets questions about payment processing architecture; a logistics company gets questions about routing and fleet management systems.

    Each question includes: **scope expectations** (what to cover in a 45-minute interview), **key tradeoffs** to address, and **likely follow-ups** to push deeper on your design decisions.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Behavioral">
    Role-agnostic behavioral questions targeting the competencies your target company is known to prioritise. Questions are mapped to the STAR stories Rolecrft already generated — each behavioral question suggests which of your stories is the best fit.

    Each question includes: **the competency being assessed**, **coaching strategy** (what interviewers are listening for), and **likely follow-ups**.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Company-Specific">
    Questions that reflect the specific company's products, business model, and known interview culture. These might cover recent product decisions, competitive positioning, or culture questions aligned with the company's stated values.

    Each question includes: **what the interviewer is actually assessing** (even if the question sounds casual), and **how to anchor your answer** to your own experience.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

### Coaching Strategies

Every question in the bank includes a coaching strategy — a one-paragraph guide explaining what the interviewer is really evaluating and how to structure an answer that addresses it directly. For behavioral questions, the coaching strategy cross-references your generated STAR stories so you know which narrative to deploy.

<Note>
  The interview question bank is most accurate for roles at large technology companies with documented interview processes. For early-stage startups with unstructured interviews, treat the question bank as a preparation guide rather than a prediction of what you'll be asked.
</Note>
