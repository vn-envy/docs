PRISM (Process Reliability Index for Supplier Models) is the first tool to apply Six Sigma Statistical Process Control to LLM evaluation. Instead of ranking models by their average score — a metric every leaderboard uses — PRISM measures **process capability**: how consistently a model stays above your minimum quality bar on every single call. A model that scores 95% on average but varies wildly is worse for production than one that scores 90% every time. PRISM gives you the statistics to prove it.

## The Core Insight

<Warning>
  **Ranking by mean score is dangerous for production systems.** Every public leaderboard ranks Model B higher in the example below. Every quality engineer ranks Model A higher. PRISM ranks like a quality engineer.
</Warning>

| Model | μ (mean) | σ (std dev) | Cpk | Verdict |
|-------|----------|-------------|-----|---------|
| Model A | 92% | 2% | **1.17** | Marginal — monitor closely |
| Model B | 95% | 8% | **0.42** | Incapable — will break in production |

Model B's high average hides a fatal flaw: its variance is so wide that a large fraction of calls will fall below any reasonable quality floor. Model A delivers fewer perfect outputs, but it almost never fails. **PRISM ranks by Cpk, not by μ.**

## Cpk Verdict Table

Cpk is the Process Capability Index — the number of standard deviations between your model's mean score and your Lower Specification Limit (LSL), divided by 3. Use this table to interpret every PRISM result.

| Cpk Range | Verdict | Meaning |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| ≥ 1.67 | **Excellent** ✨ | Six Sigma capable — world-class consistency |
| ≥ 1.33 | **Production-grade** ✅ | Safe to ship; fewer than 6,210 defects per million calls |
| ≥ 1.00 | **Marginal** ⚠️ | Monitor closely; some calls will fall below your quality bar |
| ≥ 0.67 | **Poor** 🔴 | Expect visible defects in production |
| < 0.67 | **Incapable** ❌ | Do not use; process cannot meet your specification |

## How the Measurement Works

PRISM runs a five-step pipeline automatically whenever you submit an intent:

1. **VoC → CTQ**: Your plain-English intent is translated into measurable specification limits (LSL, USL, target score per dimension).
2. **5-trial measurement**: Each candidate model runs 5 independent trials against a locked, SHA-pinned test generator.
3. **3-judge scoring**: A Gauge R&R panel (GPT-4o, GPT-4o-mini, Llama 3.3 70B) scores each output across four CTQ dimensions.
4. **Six Sigma statistics**: PRISM computes μ, σ, Cpk, DPMO, sigma level, and Gauge R&R % for each model.
5. **Bayesian MatchScore**: Results are blended with HuggingFace leaderboard priors and ranked by MatchScore descending.

<Info>
  PRISM's evaluator rubric and test generator are SHA-pinned at commit `937202df`. Evaluation criteria were locked before any model results existed, ensuring no post-hoc tuning.
</Info>
