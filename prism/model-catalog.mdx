PRISM maintains a catalog of 25 candidate models drawn from public open-weight releases and commercial APIs. Every model in the catalog has an archived HuggingFace leaderboard score for each of the four capability pillars. These scores serve as Bayesian priors — they provide a reasonable starting estimate before live trials begin, and they contribute 30% of the final MatchScore, with the remaining 70% coming from your measured evaluation.

## How HuggingFace Priors Work

When PRISM receives your intent, it selects the most relevant candidate models for live evaluation by ranking the catalog by their pillar-specific HuggingFace prior score. The top `--candidates` models (default: 5) proceed to the 5-trial measurement phase.

After measurement, the prior blends with the measured result:

```
posterior_μ = 0.30 × hf_prior_score  +  0.70 × measured_μ
```

This prevents a single unlucky evaluation run from producing a misleading recommendation and provides a safety net when API latency causes fewer than 5 trials to complete within the 90-second budget.

<Info>
  The HuggingFace archive was locked on 2026-04-19. Prior scores will not change between runs, ensuring consistent pre-selection behaviour. PRISM evaluates candidate models against your specific intent, so measured results routinely differ from generic leaderboard rankings.
</Info>

## Capability Pillars

Each model's prior score covers four pillars. PRISM uses the pillar-specific prior for pre-selection and MatchScore blending.

| Pillar | What It Measures | Example Use Case |
|--------|-----------------|-----------------|
| `structured_output` | JSON/schema compliance, field accuracy | Receipt extraction, API response generation |
| `language_fidelity` | Indic and multilingual generation quality | Hindi WhatsApp bots, regional language apps |
| `reasoning` | Multi-step logic, math, analytical tasks | Decision support, complex Q&A |
| `creative_generation` | Open-ended text quality, tone matching | Content generation, marketing copy |

## Indic Language Specialists

Two models in the catalog are purpose-built for Indian languages. When your intent contains Indic language keywords, PRISM automatically elevates these models into the candidate pool regardless of their general-purpose ranking.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Sarvam-M 24B" icon="language">
    **Provider**: Sarvam AI · **Tier**: mid · **24B parameters**

    Supports Hindi, Bengali, Tamil, Telugu, Marathi, Gujarati, Kannada, Malayalam, Punjabi, and Odia. Language fidelity prior of 89.5 — the highest in the catalog. Recommended for production Indic language applications requiring both quality and cost efficiency.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Sarvam 2B" icon="language">
    **Provider**: Sarvam AI · **Tier**: low · **2B parameters**

    Same 10-language coverage as Sarvam-M 24B at a fraction of the cost ($0.0004/1K tokens, ~400 ms latency). Language fidelity prior of 82.4. Use this model for high-volume, latency-sensitive Indic applications where cost is the primary constraint.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Warning>
  DeepSeek V3 and DeepSeek R1 both show 671B parameters in the catalog. These are Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures — only a subset of parameters is active per inference call. Their `hardware_tier: high` reflects the API cost and latency profile, not the active parameter count during inference.
</Warning>
